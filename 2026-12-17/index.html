About #

The Biofeedback & Neurofeedback Community Nijmegen (BNCNijmegen) invites you to the symposium:

Sci-Art

At this symposium, we will explore the links between bio-/neurofeedback research and art. We are delighted to welcome Stephen Whitmarsh from the Paris Brain Institute as our guest speaker. Stephen is a cognitive neuroscientist who has worked at the intersection of art and science since 2009. In his talk, he will cover collaborations between artists and scientists, the use of EEG in live performance (controlling synthesizers, sound and light), brain-computer music interfaces, and Steady-State: a project in which EEG drives rhythmic stimulation in real time. More information on Stephen can be found at stephenwhitmarsh.com. The second talk will be given by our own Robert Oostenveld, who was one of the project coordinators of the BrainGain BCI project from 2008 to 2013 and around that time also contributed to BCI2000 training workshops. In addition to his scientific work, he has developed tools and methods for neurofeedback in artistic settings. In his presentation, Robert will give his perspective on the interplay between BCI research and brain art: how scientific research has inspired artistic projects, and how, in turn, the creative and practical demands of artistic applications have driven technical innovation and contributing to new scientific insights. More information on Robert and some of his projects can be found at robertoostenveld.nl.
